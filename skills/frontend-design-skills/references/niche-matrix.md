# Niche Matrix Reference

Use this after the niche is classified in `10-art-direction.md`. Each row is an
operational system: first viewport, page shape, palette, type, proof, motion, and
the mistakes that break the row.

## Quick chooser

| Visitor is deciding… | Niche | Primary evidence |
|----------------------|-------|------------------|
| Whether the tool does the job | SaaS / devtool | Real product surface |
| Whether the model can perform the task | AI | Input and output pair |
| Whether to play or wishlist | Gaming | Key art or in-engine frame |
| Whether the object is right | Hardware | Product photograph plus spec |
| Whether to buy this item | Commerce | Product cutout plus price |
| Whether to stay or book | Hospitality | Place or room photograph |
| Whether to hire this provider | Service | Person, process, or result |
| Whether to read or subscribe | Editorial | Masthead plus lead story |

---

## 1. SaaS / devtool

**Audience.** Operators, developers, and technical buyers who will compare the
product to a trial or a competitor within minutes.

**First viewport.** UI at 6–7 columns showing the core workflow in a real state;
copy at 5 columns with category, audience, outcome, primary action; one proof
detail (integration, latency, deployment, or named customer).

**Page shape.** Hero 1H → proof strip → three capability sections (1H each, one
UI, one code, one data) → integrations 1H → pricing 1H → FAQ 1H → close 1H.

**Palette and type.** Light neutral canvas, near-black ink, one cool, green, or
amber accent. Grotesk text plus mono for code, keys, and identifiers. Density is
the signature; no lifestyle photography.

**Proof.** Live-looking UI, reproducible benchmark with method, public status
page, or named customer with a quote.

**Motion.** T1. One entrance, one UI reveal, hover states. No WebGL.

**Pitfalls.** Gray-rectangle dashboard; three equal feature cards; "enterprise"
with no security page; hidden pricing behind a sales claim; unreadable mock
labels; a screenshot that does not match the real navigation.

---

## 2. AI

**Audience.** Evaluators with a concrete task, watching for reliability, cost, and
limits.

**First viewport.** A real input/output pair or working demo; model, task, and
constraint named; one measured result with date and method, or a clear limit.

**Page shape.** Hero demo 1H → example set 2H → mechanism 1H → limits 1H →
pricing 1H → docs 1H → close 1H.

**Palette and type.** Warm off-white or deep charcoal, one signal accent, no
rainbow gradient. Neutral grotesk plus mono for model names, tokens, and code. No
robot imagery or glowing brains.

**Proof.** Reproducible examples with settings, public evaluation, latency, or a
human review process. Never invent benchmark scores.

**Motion.** T1; T2 only with a real demo and a static transcript. Demo is
pausable and reduced-motion safe.

**Pitfalls.** Chat mock with invented answers; "hallucination-free" or "AGI"
claims; a benchmark with no axis, size, or date; a gradient orb as the model;
hidden cost and rate limits.

---

## 3. Gaming

**Audience.** Players and enthusiasts scanning for atmosphere, mechanics, release
date, and platforms.

**First viewport.** One dominant frame (key art, in-engine capture, or muted
loop); title integrated with the art; release window, platforms, one action
(wishlist, preorder, play).

**Page shape.** Full-bleed hero 1H → premise strip → three asymmetric detail
blocks 2H → gallery 1H → editions 1H → requirements 1H → close 1H.

**Palette and type.** Dark base, high-chroma accent, controlled bloom. Condensed
or wide display plus a sturdy text face; accent never used for body copy.

**Proof.** In-engine footage, platform listings, release date, studio credits, or
a playable build. No fake review scores or awards.

**Motion.** T2 permitted. One signature moment: trailer-style reveal or key-art
stage on native scroll. Static poster and reduced-motion path required.

**Pitfalls.** Cinematic art that does not represent the game; autoplay audio; a
countdown with no real date; platform icons without confirmed availability; text
over busy art without a scrim.

---

## 4. Hardware

**Audience.** Buyers comparing materials, dimensions, ports, performance, and
compatibility; they need scale and precision.

**First viewport.** The object photographed well at a believable angle; headline
naming the object and defining property; one action plus spec link; a dimension
or weight near the image.

**Page shape.** Object hero 1H → three macro details 1H → spec table 1H → scale
or exploded diagram 1H → compatibility 1H → purchase 1H → close 1H.

**Palette and type.** Graphite, bone, aluminum, or one anodized accent. Precise
grotesk plus mono for measurements. Studio-neutral or environmental backgrounds,
not gradient fields.

**Proof.** Measured specs, material names, test conditions, certification, real
photography. No invented tolerances or durability numbers.

**Motion.** T1; T2 for a real 3D reveal or frame sequence. Cap WebGL at four
instances; ship a poster.

**Pitfalls.** Render presented as photograph; specs without units or conditions;
macro with no scale cue; mislabeled ports; a viewer that blocks scrolling or
fails on mobile.

---

## 5. Commerce

**Audience.** Shoppers with a product, size, compatibility, or delivery question.
They need price, availability, variants, and returns without hunting.

**First viewport.** Product isolated on a consistent ground with price and action;
variant controls visible; delivery estimate and return terms within two
viewports.

**Page shape.** Index 2H → product 1H → details 1H → delivery/returns 1H →
related 1H → footer strip.

**Palette and type.** Warm neutral or clean white; accent only for price, action,
and stock state. Humanist sans for UI; display face for campaign headers only.

**Proof.** Real inventory, delivery window, return policy, verified reviews, and
photographs of the actual item.

**Motion.** T1 at most. Image swap on variant change; no autoplay carousel; no
scroll hijacking in galleries.

**Pitfalls.** Fake countdown or "only 2 left"; photos differ from the shipped
item; hidden shipping; color-only variants; four card treatments in one grid.

---

## 6. Hospitality

**Audience.** Guests deciding on place, date, room, table, or event. They need
location, atmosphere, availability, and terms.

**First viewport.** Real photograph of the property, room, or table; name, place,
one defining sentence; booking action with date or clear availability path;
address or neighborhood within two viewports.

**Page shape.** Place hero 1H → rooms or menu 1H → location 1H → amenities 1H →
reviews strip → booking 1H → contact 1H.

**Palette and type.** Stone, clay, linen, deep green, or ink; low chroma. Serif
display plus clean sans text. Photography warm and consistent.

**Proof.** Current photography, address, hours, menu, availability, policies. No
generic resort image for a specific property.

**Motion.** T1. Slow image reveals, restrained hover; no disorienting parallax;
no autoplay video with sound.

**Pitfalls.** Photo that is not the actual room; "from" price with no date or room
type; booking widget that loses dates; menu without allergens or prices; romantic
copy that omits cancellation terms.

---

## 7. Service

**Audience.** People with a problem, a deadline, and a location. They need to know
coverage, area, and price range.

**First viewport.** Clear promise tied to a service and a place; real person,
team, or work photograph; phone or booking action; service area and hours; one
credential or result.

**Page shape.** Promise hero 1H → services 1H → process 1H → proof 1H → area 1H →
contact 1H.

**Palette and type.** Calm neutral, one grounded accent, no default tech blue.
Strong sans display plus plain text face. Documentary photography, not stock.

**Proof.** License number, insurance, years in operation, named case result,
response time, real address. No invented reviews or certifications.

**Motion.** T0 or T1. Hover and one entrance. No WebGL or parallax.

**Pitfalls.** "Trusted by thousands" with no source; a form that does not confirm
receipt; service area in the footer; stock technicians; prices omitted when the
buyer is price-sensitive.

---

## 8. Editorial

**Audience.** Readers deciding whether to spend time and attention. They need the
publication's subject, voice, and recency.

**First viewport.** Masthead or publication name; lead story with headline, deck,
byline, date, and a real image or chart; one path into the archive or
subscription.

**Page shape.** Masthead strip → lead 1H → index 1H → latest 1H → topics strip →
subscribe 1H → footer strip.

**Palette and type.** Paper neutral, ink text, one editorial accent. Serif text
with sans metadata, or sans text with serif display. Reading comfort outranks
novelty.

**Proof.** Byline, date, sources, issue number, corrections policy, real
photography or commissioned illustration.

**Motion.** T0 or T1. No entrance animation on article text; link hover only; no
scroll effects in the reading column.

**Pitfalls.** Headline that does not match the article; no date or byline; measure
above 80 characters; animation inside the article; unlabeled sponsored content.

---

## Cross-niche rules

- The row determines the proof type. Do not import a proof pattern from another
  row because it looks familiar.
- The row determines the motion ceiling. A service page never needs WebGL.
- The row determines the image language. A SaaS page with only lifestyle photos
  has no product evidence.
- If a project spans two rows, assign the row of the first viewport and document
  the secondary row in the direction lock.
- Re-check the matrix after any change to the offer, audience, or purchase mode.
